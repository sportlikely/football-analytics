# What is double chance?

A football match can end three ways: a home win (1), a draw (X) or an away win (2). A double chance covers two of those three at once, so it is right if either of them happens.

## The three double chances

- **1X**: the home team wins or the match is drawn.
- **X2**: the away team wins or the match is drawn.
- **12**: either team wins; only a draw makes it wrong.

## Why it is right more often

A single result has to land exactly. Football is low scoring and about one match in four ends level, so even good models get the exact result right only about half the time in the top leagues. A double chance leaves out one of the three outcomes, so it is right much more often. The trade-off: it says less. "Barcelona or a draw" is a safer statement than "Barcelona win".

## How we choose ours

Our model gives every match three percentages that add up to 100. Our double-chance pick is simply the two most likely results, and its percentage is their sum.

Example: Real Betis 18.5% · Draw 19.9% · Barcelona 61.6%. Our pick is **X2 (Barcelona or a draw), 81.5%**.

When a double chance reaches 85% or more, we mark it as a strong pick.

## When is it most useful?

- Close matches, where the favourite is only slightly ahead.
- Away favourites, where home advantage keeps a draw in play.
- Derbies and end-of-season games, where a point can suit both sides.

It tells you least when one team is a huge favourite: the double chance is then almost certain, but it adds little to the plain result.

## Basketball and American football

There is no double chance in basketball or American football: a basketball game always has a winner (overtime decides a tie), and an NFL tie is very rare. So our pick there is simply the **winner**, with its percentage. A strong pick is a winner at **70% or more** (in college football **75% or more**, where mismatches are more common).

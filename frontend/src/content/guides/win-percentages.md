# How to read our win percentages

## A percentage is a chance, not a promise

If we give a team 60%, then over many matches like this one that team should win about six times in ten. The other four times it won't, and that is not the model being wrong, it is football.

## The numbers add up to 100

In basketball and American football there are only two numbers, one per team (no draw): 70% for one side means 30% for the other.

## Football: three numbers

A football match has three: home win, draw and away win. Example: Monaco 52% · Draw 25% · Lille 23%. Monaco is the most likely winner, but the chance that Monaco does *not* win is still 48%.

## Honest numbers

A good model is not one that is always right; it is one whose percentages come true as often as they say. When we say 70%, it should happen about seven times in ten. We check this on every finished match.

## What counts as a strong pick

- Football: a double chance of **85% or more**, or a single result of **60% or more**.
- Basketball and the NFL: a winner at **70% or more**.
- College football: a winner at **75% or more**.

Strong picks are right more often, but none is certain.

## Why the favourite still loses

Football is low scoring: one goal, a red card or a deflection can decide a match. That is also why draws happen in roughly one game in four.

## Goals numbers

- **Over 2.5 goals**: the match has three goals or more.
- **Both teams score**: each side scores at least once.

## Numbers move before kick-off

In basketball and American football the numbers move with the injury report (and a new starting quarterback); when news changes them, the game page shows what changed and why.

## Football line-ups

When the line-ups are announced, about an hour before kick-off, we recalculate with the players who actually start. Come back then for the final numbers.
